# Sound Like a Native — Phrases, Idioms & Daily Expressions

> The gap between "correct English" and "natural English" is these phrases.
> Learn them. Use them. Sound like a native.

## Part 2: Idioms — Sorted by Meaning

### Difficulty / Effort
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Piece of cake** | Very easy | This test was a piece of cake. |
| **Hit the nail on the head** | Exactly right | You hit the nail on the head. |
| **Bite off more than you can chew** | Take on too much | Don't bite off more than you can chew. |
| **Burn the midnight oil** | Work late at night | I burned the midnight oil to finish the project. |
| **Back to square one** | Start over | The plan failed. It's back to square one. |
| **In hot water** | In trouble | He's in hot water with his boss. |
| **Bite the bullet** | Endure something painful | Just bite the bullet and do it. |

### Time & Hurry
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **In the nick of time** | Just before too late | She arrived in the nick of time. |
| **Once in a blue moon** | Very rarely | I eat junk food once in a blue moon. |
| **Around the clock** | 24 hours / non-stop | The doctors worked around the clock. |
| **In the long run** | Eventually / over time | Exercise pays off in the long run. |
| **Buy time** | Delay to gain more time | He asked questions to buy time. |

### Success & Failure
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Hit the ground running** | Start quickly and energetically | She hit the ground running on day one. |
| **Pull off** | Succeed at something difficult | I can't believe he pulled it off! |
| **Fall apart** | Break down / collapse | The deal fell apart at the last moment. |
| **Go south** | Things go wrong | The project went south quickly. |
| **Call it a day** | Stop work for the day | Let's call it a day — we're done. |

### Honesty & Secrets
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Let the cat out of the bag** | Reveal a secret | She let the cat out of the bag early. |
| **Spill the beans** | Reveal secret information | Who spilled the beans about the surprise? |
| **Straight from the horse's mouth** | From the original source | I heard it straight from the horse's mouth. |
| **Beat around the bush** | Avoid the main point | Stop beating around the bush and tell me. |

### Feelings / Emotions
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Under the weather** | Feeling sick/unwell | I'm feeling a bit under the weather today. |
| **On cloud nine** | Extremely happy | She got the promotion — she's on cloud nine. |
| **Feeling drained** | Exhausted, no energy | It was a long day. I'm feeling drained. |
| **Cold feet** | Nervous before doing something | He got cold feet before the interview. |
| **Butterflies in my stomach** | Nervous/excited feeling | I had butterflies before going on stage. |

### Agreement & Disagreement
| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **See eye to eye** | Agree completely | We don't always see eye to eye. |
| **I see it differently** | Polite disagreement | That's a good point, but I see it differently. |
| **On the same page** | Understanding each other / agreeing | Are we all on the same page? |
| **Beg to differ** | Politely disagree | I hate to beg to differ, but that's not accurate. |

## Part 3: Phrasal Verbs — Daily Use

> Phrasal verbs = verb + preposition/adverb. They change the entire meaning.

### Everyday Actions
| Phrasal Verb | Meaning | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| **wake up** | stop sleeping | I wake up at 6am. |
| **get up** | rise from bed | Come on, get up! |
| **turn on / off** | start/stop (devices) | Turn on the fan. Turn off the light. |
| **switch on / off** | same as above | Switch off your phone. |
| **pick up** | collect someone / lift | I'll pick you up at 7. |
| **drop off** | leave someone somewhere | Can you drop me off here? |
| **look up** | search for information | Look it up on Google. |
| **figure out** | understand / solve | I can't figure this out. |
| **give up** | quit / stop trying | Don't give up! |
| **run out of** | have no more left | I've run out of ideas. |
| **come up with** | think of / invent | She came up with a great idea. |
| **put off** | postpone / delay | Don't put off what you can do today. |
| **go through** | experience / review | I went through a difficult time. |
| **bring up** | mention a topic | He brought up the issue in the meeting. |
| **call off** | cancel | They called off the meeting. |
| **set up** | arrange / start | She set up a new business. |
| **fill in** | give information / substitute | Fill me in on what happened. / Can you fill in for me? |
| **hold on** | wait | Hold on a sec, I'm coming. |
| **carry on** | continue | Carry on — don't stop. |
| **back up** | support / go back | Back me up on this. |
| **break down** | stop working / lose control emotionally | My car broke down. / She broke down crying. |
| **break up** | end a relationship | They broke up last month. |
| **show up** | appear / arrive | He didn't show up. |
| **stand out** | be noticeably different | She really stands out in a crowd. |
| **take off** | leave the ground / become successful / remove clothing | The flight takes off at 9. / His career took off. / Take off your shoes. |

## Part 4: Filler Words & Conversation Flow

> Native speakers use these to sound natural while thinking.

| Filler | Use |
|--------|-----|
| **Well...** | before giving an opinion |
| **You know...** | connecting thoughts |
| **I mean...** | clarifying what you said |
| **Actually...** | correcting or adding surprising info |
| **Basically...** | summarizing simply |
| **To be honest...** | being sincere |
| **Honestly speaking...** | same as above |
| **Kind of / Sort of** | approximately / slightly |
| **Like...** | informal filler (common in casual speech) |
| **Right?** | checking agreement (like "है ना?") |
| **Fair enough.** | accepting an argument |
| **Exactly!** | strong agreement |
| **Totally!** | strong agreement (casual) |
| **That makes sense.** | understanding something |
| **That being said...** | "but on the other hand..." |

## Part 5: Agreeing, Disagreeing, Giving Opinions

### Agreeing
```
Absolutely!
Exactly!
I totally agree.
You're right.
That's a great point.
I couldn't agree more.
You hit the nail on the head.
No doubt about it.
Fair enough.
That makes sense.
```

### Disagreeing (Politely)
```
I see your point, but...
I'm not sure about that.
I beg to differ.
I see it differently.
That's interesting, but in my view...
With all due respect, I think...
I understand where you're coming from, but...
```

### Giving Your Opinion
```
In my opinion...
Personally, I think...
As far as I'm concerned...
From my point of view...
If you ask me...
The way I see it...
To be honest...
I believe that...
```

## Part 6: Starting & Ending Conversations

### Starting
```
Hey, how's it going?
What's up?
How have you been?
Long time no see!
Good to see you.
What's new?
```

### Keeping it going
```
That's interesting — tell me more.
I know exactly what you mean.
What do you think about...?
Have you ever...?
Speaking of which...
By the way...
```

### Ending
```
It was great talking to you.
Let's catch up soon.
I'll be in touch.
Take care!
Catch you later.
Take it easy.
Talk soon.
```

## Part 7: Must-Know Expressions for Work/Interview

| Situation | Expression |
|-----------|-----------|
| Asking for clarification | "Could you elaborate on that?" |
| Buying thinking time | "That's a great question. Let me think about that." |
| Adding to what someone said | "Building on that point..." |
| Admitting you don't know | "I'm not entirely sure, but I believe..." |
| Agreeing with your boss | "Absolutely, that makes total sense." |
| Disagreeing professionally | "I understand your perspective. My concern is..." |
| Presenting an idea | "I'd like to propose that we..." |
| Wrapping up | "To summarize..." / "In conclusion..." |
| Moving to next topic | "Moving on..." / "On another note..." |

## Memory Trick — Group by Body Part 🧠

```
MOUTH:
  Bite the bullet       → endure pain
  Bite off more than you can chew → take too much

EYES:
  See eye to eye        → agree
  Turn a blind eye      → ignore intentionally

EARS:
  I'm all ears          → I'm listening
  Play it by ear        → decide as you go

HEART:
  Have a heart          → be kind
  Lose heart            → become discouraged

HANDS:
  Could use a hand      → need help
  Give a hand           → applaud / help

FOOT/FEET:
  Cold feet             → nervous
  Put your foot in it   → say something embarrassing/wrong
  Get off on the right foot → start well
```
