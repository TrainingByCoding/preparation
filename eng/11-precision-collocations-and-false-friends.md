# Precision Collocations and False Friends

> Small word choices make a huge difference in spoken English quality.
> This file fixes high-frequency errors from social media and daily speech.

---

## 1. Correct Collocations (Say It Naturally)

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| Where are the keys of my car? | Where are the keys to my car? |
| She did a request. | She made a request. |
| I came by walk. | I came on foot. |
| Do a party | Have a party / throw a party |
| Give an exam (student) | Take an exam |
| Give an exam (teacher) | Administer an exam |
| Return back | Return |
| Discuss about | Discuss |
| Order for food | Order food |
| Explain me this | Explain this to me |

---

## 2. Make vs Do: Advanced Usage

### Use MAKE for outcomes/results
- make a request
- make a promise
- make progress
- make an effort
- make a complaint
- make a reservation

### Use DO for tasks/activities
- do homework
- do research
- do the dishes
- do your best
- do business

Mini-check:
1. She ___ a complaint yesterday. -> made
2. I need to ___ some research. -> do
3. We ___ good progress this week. -> made

---

## 3. Keys To, Access To, Solution To (Not "of")

Use "to" with these nouns:
- key to success
- answer to a question
- solution to a problem
- approach to learning
- invitation to the event

Examples:
- What is the key to fluent speaking?
- Do you have access to this document?

---

## 4. Disinterested vs Uninterested

This is a high-level vocabulary distinction.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| disinterested | impartial, unbiased | A judge must be disinterested. |
| uninterested | not interested, bored | He seemed uninterested in the topic. |

Common error:
- Wrong: The judges should be uninterested.
- Right: The judges should be disinterested.

Memory trick:
- disinterested = detached from personal gain
- uninterested = no curiosity

## 5. Verb + Preposition Precision

| Verb | Correct preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| depend | on | It depends on traffic. |
| apply | for | I applied for the role. |
| apply | to | This rule applies to everyone. |
| listen | to | Listen to me. |
| participate | in | She participated in the debate. |
| insist | on | He insisted on accuracy. |
| focus | on | We should focus on outcomes. |
| object | to | I object to that proposal. |

---

## 6. Professional Correction Lines

Instead of bluntly saying "wrong," use:
- I think a more natural phrase is...
- A better collocation here is...
- In standard usage, we usually say...
- You are close; one small correction...

Examples:
- We usually say "make a request," not "do a request."
- We generally say "on foot," not "by walk."

---

## 7. Error Conversion Drill

Convert to natural English:
1. She did a request to the manager.
2. Where are the keys of my car?
3. I came to office by walk.
4. The committee must be uninterested.
5. Please discuss about this issue.

Answers:
1. She made a request to the manager.
2. Where are the keys to my car?
3. I came to the office on foot.
4. The committee must be disinterested.
5. Please discuss this issue.
