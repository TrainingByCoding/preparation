# Modal Verbs — The 9 Power Words

> **Modal verbs** never change form (no -s, -ing, -ed). They're always followed by **base verb**.
> Formula: `Subject + Modal + Base Verb`

---

## Quick Reference Cheat Sheet

| Modal | Core Meaning | Time | Example |
|-------|-------------|------|---------|
| **can** | ability (present) | now | I **can** swim. |
| **could** | ability (past) / polite request | past | I **could** swim at age 5. |
| **may** | permission (formal) / possibility | now/future | **May** I come in? |
| **might** | weak possibility | now/future | It **might** rain today. |
| **must** | necessity / strong obligation | now | You **must** stop here. |
| **should** | advice / right thing to do | now/future | You **should** sleep early. |
| **would** | future-in-past / polite request | past habit | She said she **would** come. |
| **shall** | offers / formal future (I/We) | future | **Shall** we begin? |
| **will** | certain future / decision now | future | I **will** call you. |

---

## 1. CAN — Ability (Present)

**Use it when:** You are able to do something RIGHT NOW.

```
✅ I can speak spanish.
✅ She can drive a car.
✅ They can answer the question.
❌ I can to swim.       ← WRONG (no "to" after modal)
❌ She cans cook.       ← WRONG (no -s on modal)
```

**Negative:** `cannot` / `can't`
```
I can't understand this.
He cannot come today.
```

**Question:** `Can + subject + base verb?`
```
Can you help me?
Can she play guitar?
```

### Real-Life Scenarios
| Situation | Sentence |
|-----------|----------|
| Job interview | "I can work under pressure." |
| Asking for help | "Can you repeat that?" |
| Describing skill | "I can code in Go." |

---

## 2. COULD — Past Ability + Polite Requests

**Two main uses:**

### Use A: Past Ability (what you were able to do before)
```
✅ I could go to school every day.       ← past habit/ability
✅ They could answer the question.
✅ She could run 10km when she was young.
```
> Think: "could" = "can" but in the past

### Use B: Polite Request (softer than "can")
```
✅ Could you please help me?             ← more polite than "Can you?"
✅ Could I use your phone?
✅ Could you explain this again?
```

### Use C: Possibility (present/future)
```
✅ It could rain later.
✅ This could be the answer.
```

**Negative:** `couldn't` / `could not`
```
I couldn't sleep last night.
She couldn't understand the question.
```

### CAN vs COULD — Side by Side
| | CAN | COULD |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Present | Past / Polite |
| Certainty | More certain | Less certain / Softer |
| Example | I can help you. | I could help you (if you want). |

---

## 3. MAY — Permission (Formal) + Possibility

### Use A: Asking/Giving Permission (FORMAL)
```
✅ May I come in?              ← formal, respectful
✅ May I use your pen?
✅ You may leave now.          ← giving permission
```
> **Rule:** Use "may" with teacher, boss, elders — it's formal

### Use B: Possibility
```
✅ It may rain tomorrow.
✅ She may come to the party.
✅ I may be wrong.
```

**Negative:** `may not`
```
You may not enter this area.   ← prohibition (formal)
It may not work.               ← possibility of not happening
```

### MAY vs CAN — Asking Permission
| | Formality | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **May** | Formal | May I use the restroom? |
| **Can** | Informal | Can I use the restroom? |

---

## 4. MIGHT — Weak Possibility

**Use it when:** Something is possible but not very likely (less sure than "may")

```
✅ It might rain today.           ← 30-40% chance
✅ I might be late.
✅ She might know the answer.
✅ We might go to the movies.
```

**Negative:** `might not`
```
It might not work.
He might not come.
```

### MAY vs MIGHT — Certainty Level
```
Certainty scale:
Will (90%) > May (50-60%) > Might (30-40%)

"It will rain."   → very sure
"It may rain."    → somewhat sure
"It might rain."  → not very sure
```

---

## 5. MUST — Strong Necessity / Obligation

**Two main uses:**

### Use A: Strong Obligation / Necessity
```
✅ You must go home.             ← no choice
✅ You must wear a seatbelt.
✅ I must finish this today.
```

### Use B: Strong Deduction / Conclusion
```
✅ He must be tired.             ← I'm sure he is tired (logical conclusion)
✅ This must be the right answer.
✅ She must know him — they're talking like old friends.
```

**Negative:** `must not` / `mustn't` = PROHIBITION (forbidden!)
```
You must not smoke here.        ← it's forbidden
You mustn't touch that wire.
```

> ⚡ **MUST NOT ≠ DON'T HAVE TO**
> - `must not` = it's FORBIDDEN (don't do it!)
> - `don't have to` = it's NOT NECESSARY (but you can if you want)

```
You must not lie.               ← lying is forbidden
You don't have to come early.   ← coming early isn't required
```

---

## 6. SHOULD — Advice / Right Thing To Do

**Use it when:** Giving advice, recommendations, or saying what is correct/expected.

```
✅ You should watch the video.
✅ They should respect elders.
✅ We should eat healthy food.
✅ You should learn English.
✅ You should sleep 8 hours a day.
```

**Negative:** `shouldn't` / `should not`
```
You shouldn't eat too much sugar.
They shouldn't be late.
```

**Question:** `Should + subject + base verb?`
```
Should I call her?
Should we leave now?
```

### SHOULD vs MUST — Strength
```
"You must stop."      → no choice, very strong
"You should stop."    → advice, you have a choice
```

### Real-Life Uses
```
Giving advice:      "You should see a doctor."
Making a suggestion: "We should try that restaurant."
Saying what's right: "People should be kind."
```

---

## 7. WOULD — Future in the Past + Polite Requests

**Three main uses:**

### Use A: Future in the Past
> When you're talking about the past and something was planned/expected to happen AFTER that past moment.

```
✅ You promised me that you would buy me a new car.
✅ She told me that she would come to the party.
✅ He said he would call me.
✅ I knew it would rain.
```
> Think: Someone said/thought/promised something in the past about the future

### Use B: Polite Requests (softer than "will")
```
✅ Would you please close the door?
✅ Would you like some tea?
✅ Would you mind helping me?
```

### Use C: Past Habits / Repeated Actions in the Past
```
✅ When I was a child, I would wake up early.
✅ Every summer, we would visit our grandparents.
```

**Negative:** `wouldn't` / `would not`
```
He said he wouldn't help.
She wouldn't listen to me.
```

### WILL vs WOULD — Formality & Time
| | Time | Formality | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **will** | Now → Future | Normal | I will help you. |
| **would** | Past → Future / Polite | More polite | I would like to help. |

---

## 8. SHALL — Offers & Formal Future

```
✅ Shall we begin?              ← offering to start
✅ Shall I open the window?     ← offering to do something
✅ I shall return.              ← formal/literary future
```
> Mainly used with **I** and **We**. Rare in American English but common in British English.

---

## 9. WILL — Certain Future / Instant Decision

```
✅ I will call you tonight.     ← future plan
✅ It will be sunny tomorrow.   ← prediction
✅ I'll have the pasta, please. ← instant decision
```

## The Big Picture — All Together

```
ABILITY:
  Present → CAN     "I can code."
  Past    → COULD   "I could code when I was 10."

POSSIBILITY:
  Strong  → MAY     "It may rain." (50%)
  Weak    → MIGHT   "It might rain." (30%)

OBLIGATION:
  Strong  → MUST    "You must stop." (no choice)
  Soft    → SHOULD  "You should stop." (advice)

FUTURE:
  Certain  → WILL    "I will go."
  In Past  → WOULD   "I said I would go."
  Polite   → WOULD   "Would you like tea?"

PERMISSION:
  Formal   → MAY     "May I leave?"
  Informal → CAN     "Can I leave?"
```

---

## Common Mistakes — Watch Out!

```
❌ She musts go home.           → ✅ She must go home.
❌ You should to eat well.      → ✅ You should eat well.
❌ I can to speak English.      → ✅ I can speak English.
❌ He might is late.            → ✅ He might be late.
❌ Could you to help me?        → ✅ Could you help me?
❌ She wills come tomorrow.     → ✅ She will come tomorrow.
```

> **Golden Rule:** Modal + BASE VERB (never add "to", never add -s/-ing/-ed)

## Quick-Fire Practice 🔥

Fill in the blank with the right modal:

1. I ___ swim when I was 5. (past ability)
2. ___ I use your charger? (formal permission)
3. It ___ rain later, bring an umbrella. (weak possibility)
4. You ___ eat more vegetables. (advice)
5. You ___ not smoke here. (prohibition)
6. She told me she ___ be here by 8. (future in past)
7. He ___ be tired — he worked 12 hours. (logical deduction)
8. ___ you help me with this? (polite request)

**Answers:** 1.could  2.May  3.might  4.should  5.must  6.would  7.must  8.Could/Would
